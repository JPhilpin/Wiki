The Whole Earth Catalog was an American counterculture magazine and product catalogue. Brand published it several times a year from 1968 to 1972 and occasionally until 1998. It was mostly product reviews, with essays around them, and its editorial focus was self-sufficiency, ecology, alternative education, do it yourself and holism. It reviewed everything from farming equipment to cybernetics texts, in an oversized format of 11 by 14 inches. Steve Jobs compared it to Google in paperback form, decades before Google existed.

## The editions

The Last Whole Earth Catalog won the National Book Award in Contemporary Affairs and sold 1.5 million copies. Across all editions it sold more than 2.5 million. Later editions include the Whole Earth Epilog and the Millennium Whole Earth Catalog.

## What grew from it

A family of journals and magazines descended from the Catalog and ran until 2002: CoEvolution Quarterly, Whole Earth Software Review, Whole Earth Review and Whole Earth Magazine.

## The archive

The Whole Earth Index is a nonprofit, nearly complete archive of the publications, made available for scholarship, education and research.
