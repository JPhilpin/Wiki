David McCandless is a London-based data-journalist and information designer who pioneered the practice of turning overwhelming datasets into beautiful, story-driven visualisations. His work has reshaped how journalists, designers, and analysts approach the problem of information overload—demonstrating that clarity and beauty are not opposing forces, but complementary ones.

**McCandless's contribution to Structured Thought is the insight that information design is knowledge compression.** In his TED talk and through Information Is Beautiful, he argues that visualising information is how we escape data glut—by seeing patterns and connections that matter, designing information so that it tells a story or highlights what is important, we squeeze enormous amounts of information and understanding into a form we can grasp. This is Structured Thought in action: making the invisible visible by squeezing signal from noise.

His approach combines rigorous data work with design sensibility, treating datasets not as abstract numbers but as stories waiting to be found. By combining disparate datasets—military spending, media buzz, social media behaviour—McCandless reveals unexpected patterns and relationships that change how we see the world. His work has appeared in over forty publications internationally, and has been exhibited at the Museum of Modern Art in New York, Tate Britain, and the Wellcome Trust gallery in London.

His books Information Is Beautiful, Knowledge Is Beautiful, and Beautiful News extend this discipline across different domains—visual, factual, journalistic—all grounded in the principle that information, when made visible intelligently, becomes a form of knowledge that ordinary people can understand, trust, and act upon.
