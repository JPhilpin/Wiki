## Working definition

A durable system is several systems stacked on top of each other, each changing at its own speed. The model makes visible that the fast layers attract the attention while the slow layers hold the power, and that the friction between them is what keeps the whole system healthy.

## Provenance

Borrowed from [[Stewart Brand]], who is explicit that the idea passed through several hands.

- Early 1970s: the architect Frank Duffy describes a building as layers of longevity: Shell, Services, Scenery and Set.
- 1994: Brand expands Duffy's four layers to six for *How Buildings Learn*: Site, Structure, Skin, Services, Space Plan and Stuff.
- December 1996: Brand sketches the civilisation version after working it through with [[Brian Eno]]. Eno's input changed the top layer from Art to Fashion and the Government layer to Governance.
- 1999: the diagram debuts in *[[Clock of the Long Now|The Clock of the Long Now]]*.
- 2018: Brand publishes the fullest statement, "Pace Layering: How Complex Systems Learn and Keep Learning", in the Journal of Design and Science.

Brand also credits Freeman Dyson, among others, for helping him form the concept.

## The model

### The six layers

Brand names six layers, from fastest to slowest.

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| Fashion and art | Froth: quick, experimental and self-preoccupied. It supplies the energy for commerce and the occasional idea that sifts down into slower layers. |
| [[Commerce]] | Exploits and absorbs shocks. It may instruct the layers below but must not control them, because it is short-sighted. |
| Infrastructure | Transport, communication, education and science. The payback is too long for ordinary investment, so governance and culture have to carry the cost. |
| Governance | Rules and the public sector, plus the social sector of nonprofits that serve the larger, slower good. |
| [[Culture]] | Moves at the pace of language and religion, over centuries. The work of whole peoples. |
| Nature | The slowest layer and the most powerful. A drought ended the world's first empire. |

### How the layers behave

Fast layers learn and propose. Slow layers remember and decide. The small, fast layers instruct the big, slow ones through accumulated innovation and the occasional revolution. The big, slow layers control the small, fast ones through constraint and constancy. Brand's summary: "Fast gets all our attention, slow has all the power."

Each layer does a different job, works somewhat independently and responds mainly to the layers next to it. That is what makes the whole resilient. Some parts take the shock quickly so that slower parts can ignore it and carry on with continuity. Brand's ecological picture is a conifer forest, where needle, crown, patch, stand, forest and biome form a time hierarchy as well as a size one, from about a year for the needle to ten thousand years for the biome.

[[Paul Saffo]], a futurist and founding board member of [[Long Now|The Long Now Foundation]], uses and teaches the model, including in his forecasting course at Stanford. He calls the friction between layers "constructive turbulence".

### What goes wrong

Trouble starts when one layer is forced to move at another layer's pace. Commerce pushing nature at a commercial pace costs forests, fisheries and aquifers. Changing governance suddenly rather than gradually produced the French and Russian revolutions. The Soviet Union forced a five-year-plan pace on commerce and art while ignoring the constraints of culture and nature. That cut it off from both support and innovation, and it did not survive.

### Reading it for organisations

Brand built the model for civilisation, and the essay does not carry it into organisations. The wiki takes that step. Different parts of an organisation run at incompatible speeds, and most change programmes try to move everything at once. The test question is: which layer does this change live in, and does the plan respect that layer's pace?

### Limits

Brand himself describes the diagram as free of data. That is why it travels: people have applied it to design, management, engineering and software as well as civilisation. It is also why it proves nothing. It is a lens for asking which layer a thing lives in and how fast that layer moves, not a measurement.
