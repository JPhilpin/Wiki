# Personal

🔴 written - not read

> [!quote] Every framework applies to organisations. This one applies to you.

> [!question] You might think it's...
> A productivity system. A journalling app. Another way to organise your to-do list.
>
> It isn't. It's the same questions an organisation asks about itself, applied to one person: what do you actually need to follow, who are the people that matter, what do you know versus what are you just storing, and where are you, really.

## What It Means

Most people never ask those questions systematically. They operate on habit, on instinct, on whatever happened to land in front of them today, which is fine until the noise drowns the signal, the connections go unmaintained, the knowledge disappears, and the picture of where you actually are gets blurry. Personal applies the domains framework to your own life, not as a system to manage tasks, but as a structured way of thinking about what you're doing and why.

## The Full Picture

You are not an organisation. But you have a domain, and it deserves the same clarity. Intelligence at the personal level means knowing what you need to follow and filtering everything else. Pulse means tracking the things that matter to you over time, not once, but as a trend. Assembly means maintaining the relationships that compound rather than the ones that merely accumulate. Library means holding what you've learned in a form you can actually use. Reveal means being honest about where you are, not where you wish you were.

None of this requires software. It requires structure, and the willingness to look at your own situation with the same rigour you'd bring to a client engagement. That's harder than it sounds. It's also more useful than almost anything else you could do.

## How It Fits

Personal is the domain that doesn't appear on any org chart, and it's also the one that determines how well everything else works. Get this right and the other seven domains compound. Leave it unexamined and they fragment, because every other domain is being run, ultimately, by a person who hasn't applied the same discipline to themselves. *(Provisional. # Definitions

## One Sentence

Personal applies the same domains framework an organisation uses on itself to one person's own life, with the same clarity and the same rigour.

## One Paragraph

Personal isn't a productivity system. It's the same structured questions an organisation asks about itself, asked of one person instead: what actually needs following, who the relationships that matter are, what's genuinely known versus what's just been stored, and whether where you are matches where you think you are. None of it needs software. It needs the willingness to look at your own situation as rigorously as you'd look at a client's.

## The Long Paragraph

Most people never ask these questions systematically. They run on habit and whatever happened to land in front of them that day, which works fine until the noise drowns the signal, the relationships go unmaintained, the knowledge disappears, and the picture of where things actually stand gets blurry. Personal takes the same framework built for organisations and applies it at the individual level: what to follow and what to filter, what to track over time rather than once, which relationships compound rather than merely accumulate, what's actually been learned versus what's just been collected, and an honest read on where things stand rather than where you'd like them to be. It's the domain that doesn't show up on any org chart, and the one that quietly determines how well every other domain performs.
