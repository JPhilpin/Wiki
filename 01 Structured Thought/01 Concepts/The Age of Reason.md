# The Age of Reason

**The Age of Reason** describes a way of thinking in which the world is made understandable by dividing it into categories, processes, hierarchies and repeatable structures.

That approach enabled extraordinary progress. Standardisation, measurement, specialisation and rational analysis made large institutions, industrial production and scalable systems possible.

Its limitation appears when the structures used to explain reality become more important than the reality itself. Complex relationships are flattened into boxes, changing contexts are treated as fixed, and uncertainty is forced into models designed for predictability.

Structured Thought does not reject reason. It recognises that reason remains necessary but is no longer sufficient on its own for a world characterised by networks, abundance, rapid change and continuous interaction.

The **Age of Experience** names the emerging context in which those additional dimensions matter.
