**Website:** [longnow.org](https://longnow.org/)

**Founded by:** [[Stewart Brand]] and Danny Hillis

**Named by:** [[Brian Eno]]

The Long Now Foundation exists to foster long-term thinking and responsibility. Brand's diagnosis was that civilisation was accelerating into a pathologically short attention span, and long-term thinking is the corrective. Ten thousand years is about the age of modern civilisation, so imagining ten thousand years ahead sets the timespan the Foundation calls the long now.

## What it asks of people

The Foundation's vision is a civilisation of good ancestors, whose members see themselves in the middle of the story and as someone else's ancestor. It offers seven principles for working at that scale:

- Serve the long view
- Foster responsibility
- Reward patience
- Mind mythic depth
- Ally with competition
- Consider all sides
- Leverage longevity

## Brian Eno

[[Brian Eno]] is a co-founder and board member of the Foundation, and he named it. His essay "The Big Here and Long Now" is one of the two essays behind Brand's book *[[Clock of the Long Now|The Clock of the Long Now]]*. Danny Hillis's essay on the millennium clock is the other. Eno's point is that the present is never just a moment: it grows out of the past and seeds the future. He also holds that civilisations with a longer now look after things better.

## The Foundation's projects

- **The 10,000 Year Clock** - A mechanical monument, designed by Danny Hillis, built to keep time for ten millennia. Under construction inside a mountain in West Texas.
- **The Rosetta Project** - An effort to preserve human languages for the long term.
- **[[Long Bets]]** - A public arena for accountable predictions.
- **Long Now Talks** - A lecture series that began as SALT, Seminars About Long-term Thinking. More than 400 speakers so far.
- **The Interval** - The Foundation's bar, cafe, exhibit space and event venue in San Francisco.

## Where it meets Pace Layers

Brand opens his Pace Layers essay with Eno's remark about civilisations with long nows, then offers the layers as the mechanism behind it: fast layers absorb the shock so that slow layers can carry on with their steady duties. The Pace Layers diagram first appeared in *[[Clock of the Long Now|The Clock of the Long Now]]*. See [[Pace Layers]] for the model.
