# The Age of Experience

**The Age of Experience** describes an emerging context in which reason remains essential but is no longer sufficient.

Information is abundant, context changes continuously, and people experience organisations, products and institutions through networks of interactions rather than through isolated transactions. Meaning therefore depends increasingly on relationships, nuance, feedback and the circumstances in which something occurs.

Where the **Age of Reason** favoured boxes, standardisation and linear structures, the Age of Experience requires structures that can accommodate emergence, non-linearity and multiple perspectives without abandoning clarity.

This shift has practical consequences. Organisations must listen as well as broadcast, learn as well as plan, and understand experience as a source of information rather than treating people simply as endpoints in a process.

Structured Thought is designed to operate in this environment: enough structure to make complexity intelligible, without pretending that complexity has disappeared.
