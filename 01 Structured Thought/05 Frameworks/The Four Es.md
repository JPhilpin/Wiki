# The Four Es

## The Four Ps, and why they stop working

The Four Ps — Product, Price, Place, Promotion — describe a seller pushing a fixed offer into a passive market. They assume the business decides what exists, what it costs, where it is found, and how the market hears about it. That model suited an [[The Age of Reason|Age of Reason]] world: population thinking, standard offers, broadcast media, a clear boundary between seller and buyer.

That is not the world we are in. This is the [[The Age of Experience|Age of Experience]] — the age of engagement — where the customer already knows more than the seller assumes, moves through a private arc long before any seller is in the room (see [[Engagement Framework]]), and judges a business by what it is like to deal with, not by what it is told to think. The Four Ps have nothing to say about any of that. No P describes why someone should care, what they are actually giving up to engage, how much friction stands in their way, or what the relationship feels like once it is underway.

## The Four Es

The Four Es are not a rebrand of the Four Ps. They are a structural shift in how growth actually works — replacing seller-side categories with customer-side ones.

**Essence** replaces Product. Who you are and why you matter — not the feature list, the reason a customer would care in the first place.

**Exchange** replaces Price. The mutual value created, not just money changing hands: time, trust and attention are all currency a customer spends to engage with you.

**Ease** replaces Place. How frictionless it is to find you, use you, and stay — availability is no longer a shelf position, it is the absence of friction at every step.

**Experience** replaces Promotion. Every touchpoint either reinforces or erodes the relationship. You do not announce yourself once and walk away; the relationship is being made or unmade continuously.

## Where it applies

Essence, Exchange, Ease and Experience are not stages — they are a lens applied at every stage of [[Engagement Framework]], the same way TAM/SAM/SOM narrows population and ownership marks internal accountability. Essence dominates at Notice — does the customer even know why you would matter. Ease and Exchange dominate at Engage and Commit. Experience dominates from Embed onward, compounding back into Essence at Advocate: a customer who has had a good Experience is the one who repeats your Essence back to other people, unprompted.
