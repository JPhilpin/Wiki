## Introduction

This page applies Structured Thought - the domain thinking and the architecture map behind PHI⑊PIN - to the structure of an organisation, with a people-first focus. It describes how an organisation can run on agreements rather than rules, so that the way people work together inside it matches the way it engages with everyone outside it.

The map below is where it starts. Everything customers see sits above the ground line; everything that makes it possible sits below. This framework is the foundation band: **PHI, the people-first solution**.

![[architecture-philpin.svg]]

Three notes for reading the map alongside this framework:

- **The outer arc and the five states are different things.** The arc - clarity, confidence, trust, momentum, advantage, growth - is what customers experience as impact. The five states - clarity, ownership, trust, wellbeing, knowledge - are what holds true inside the organisation. Inside is what makes the outside possible.
- **State · Flow** in the Engagement band is the same idea used here: every state needs a flow that keeps it true.
- **Assembly** on the diagram is the Domain where groups of people gather. It is where agreements are renewed, and its conveners are whoever brings people together.

## Quick take

**We don't have rules. We have agreements, and we keep them.**

Organisations built for the age of record run on rules: written once, handed down, enforced. The age of engagement runs on agreements: made together, kept by everyone, renewed as things change. This framework starts with purpose, works through five flows - how we write, decide, behave, care and measure - and the five states those flows keep true. Culture, leadership and performance are not written down. They show up when the flows keep running.

## Purpose, mission, vision, values

Each of these words is useless until it can be seen.

| Word | It isn't | It is | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | A statement on the wall | The reason the work matters this week | Above everything: why the flows run at all |
| Mission | A slogan | What we do, for whom, in one sentence we can check | The job the five flows are pointed at |
| Vision | A future that never arrives | The states we hold true, at the scale we are heading for | The five states, grown |
| Values | What we say | What we do when no one is checking | The agreements |

The test for all four: read it back aloud to someone outside the organisation. If it could belong to anyone, it doesn't belong to us.

## The people-first story

The age of record ran on contracts signed once and enforced. The age of engagement runs on agreements made together and renewed constantly. Most organisations run their people the way they run their data: policies filed, handbooks issued, surveys collected. That is culture as a [[Systems of Record|system of record]].

| | Rules | Contracts | Social contract | Cooperative agreements | Sharing models |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who makes it | Imposed | Signed once | Assumed, unwritten | Made together | Built together |
| How it lasts | Enforced | Filed | Inherited | Renewed | Earned, again and again |
| Who gains | The organisation | Both, on paper | Everyone, vaguely | The members | Everyone the value reaches |
| Era | Record | Record | The bridge | Engagement | Engagement |

- **The social contract is the whole framework.** It was always there; engagement writes it down and keeps it current.
- **Agreements are its parts.** An agreement nobody engages with goes stale and hardens back into a rule.
- **Users consume. Members contribute.** The people outside - customers, partners, community - are members with a stake.

**A rule is a contract you didn't sign. An agreement is one you keep renewing.**

## How the model works

Purpose sets the direction. Two frames hold everything:

- **We: us + them, inside and outside.** Whatever we promise each other, we promise our members too. Us *and* them is a battle, two sides facing each other. Us *+* them adds up to one we.
- **People and agents together.** Every agreement says how it holds when an agent did the work.

Inside the frames, five flows each keep one state true. Performance is what comes out. How we measure loops back into how we write, which is how *if we say it, we do it* is kept.

| Flow: what we do | State: what holds true |
| --- | --- |
| How we write | Clarity: everyone understands |
| How we decide | Ownership: everyone knows who chooses |
| How we behave | Trust: what we say matches what we do |
| How we care | Wellbeing: time and lives are protected |
| How we measure | Knowledge: what is working, and what is coming |

## State and flow

Every state needs a flow that keeps it true. A state with no flow is a platitude.

- "We are" is a state. "We will be" is a wish.
- A dashboard shows a state: where we have been. An alert shows a flow: where we are going.
- A policy is a state. The flow that keeps it true is the promise.

## The agreements

Each agreement has three parts: the state it keeps, the flow that keeps it, and how it holds when an agent did the work.

### How we write - Clarity

| Agreement | State | Flow | With agents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| We are | We write in the present tense | Every doc gets an edit pass that strikes out "will" and "should" | Agent drafts get the same pass before they are shared |
| Only people think | The organisation doesn't care, expect or think; we do | "The company should" becomes "we" | Agents never speak as "the company"; a named person stands behind what they write |
| We write short, slowly | What we write can be read | One person writes it slowly; another cuts it before it goes out | Agents draft long; people cut |
| Shorts first | A short is our default | Detail only on request | Agent summaries are shorts by default |
| Three levels | Everything has a headline, a paragraph, a page | No headline, no publish | Agents produce all three; a person checks the headline |
| No platitudes | Every line says how it happens | Review asks "how?" of every line | Generated text faces the same test |
| Examples, not nouns | Every value carries a real example | Examples refreshed quarterly from real work | Examples come from real events, never generated |
| We read it back | Every value is true of us today | Read it aloud to an outsider; rewrite if they don't believe it | An agent cannot read it back for us |
| One page, one job | Philosophy, policy and backlog live apart | Open questions move to the backlog | Same |

### How we decide - Ownership

| Agreement | State | Flow | With agents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The owner decides | Every decision has an owner | A map of who decides what, reviewed when roles change | Agents recommend; people decide |
| We write it down | Decisions are logged | Logged within a day; unlogged means undecided | An agent may write the entry; the owner signs it |
| Lowest level | Decisions sit closest to the work | Escalate early, to a named person | An agent escalates to a person, never only to another agent |
| A no has a reason | Every no is explained | A no is logged with its reason, like a yes | An agent's refusal carries its reason |
| No matters | The won't-do list is as visible as the will-do list | Each decision records what we said no to | Same |

### How we behave - Trust

| Agreement | State | Flow | With agents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| We do more than we say | Behaviour matches words | Measuring compares what we said with what we did | Agent actions are logged and compared the same way |
| If we say it, we do it | Every commitment has an owner and a date | Reviewed at the next check-in; dropped openly, never quietly | A commitment an agent makes is owned by a person |
| We engage | We stay in touch with everyone, whoever they are | A steady rhythm of check-ins and open channels | Agents can prompt the rhythm; they don't replace the conversation |
| We ask | Questions are welcome everywhere | Every update ends with a question | Agents ask rather than guess |
| Interested, not interesting | We listen more than we broadcast | Conversations start with the other person's question | Agents surface what others said before what we said |
| Us + them = we | The same promises hold inside and out | Every internal agreement gets the test: "and for our members?" | Agents treat members as we treat each other |
| No more forms | Feedback is acted on, not collected | "You said, we did" closes every input | Agents close the loop; people own the answer |

### How we care - Wellbeing

| Agreement | State | Flow | With agents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Our time is protected | Agreed hours are the hours | Scope is cut each cycle to fit | Agents take load off; never a reason to add more |
| Out-of-hours work is real work | Planned, paid and shared | A rota, time off in lieu, a review after each stretch | Agents cover first; people only when needed |
| Off means off | No contact out of hours unless on shift | Rota, handover, messages scheduled for working hours | Agents hold messages until working hours |
| We grow people | Everyone has a path | Onboarding and regular development conversations | Including growing how we work with agents |
| Care is material | Pay, time and tools come first | Reviewed before any perks | Good agents are among the tools we provide |

### How we measure - Knowledge

| Agreement | State | Flow | With agents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alerts over dashboards | The dashboard shows where we are | Alerts flag trends before they arrive | Agents watch the trends; people decide |
| We measure soft things | Value and quality are tracked | Stories reviewed alongside the numbers | Agents gather; people judge |
| People over numbers | Impact is shown through voices | One real story collected each cycle | Real voices, never generated |

### Keeping the agreements alive

| Agreement | State | Flow | With agents |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| We renew these agreements | The set is current and believed | No fixed rhythm: anyone can convene an assembly whenever an agreement stops being true | Agents can flag an agreement drifting from practice |

## How we transform

Transformation is a flow, not a project: it starts small, runs constantly, and never finishes.

1. **Name the purpose.** One line, in the people's own words. Read it back.
2. **Take stock of the states.** For each of the five, what is actually true today? Ask the people.
3. **Write the agreements.** For each state we want, the flow that keeps it true, and how it holds with agents.
4. **Start with one flow.** The one where the gap between say and do is widest.
5. **Measure and renew.** Alerts, not just dashboards. When an agreement stops being true, anyone convenes an assembly to change it.
6. **Turn outward.** Extend the same agreements to members outside. Inside and outside are one system.

The organisation's own documents are the raw material for steps 2 and 3, each rewritten at three levels.
