## The Atomic Principle

An atomic note follows one core rule: one idea per note. Just as atoms are the indivisible units of matter, atomic notes are the indivisible units of thought. This might be a single insight, observation, argument, or question - but never multiple unrelated concepts bundled together.

This constraint is powerful. When each note stands alone, it becomes infinitely reusable. You can link it to different contexts, weave it into various arguments, and discover connections you never anticipated when you first wrote it.
## Why Atomic Matters

Traditional note-taking often creates monolithic documents - meeting notes, book summaries, project files - that trap ideas inside specific contexts. Finding that one brilliant insight months later means sifting through pages of unrelated material.

Atomic notes invert this. Each idea exists independently, tagged and linked to related concepts. The same note about "emergence in complex systems" might connect to your thoughts on urban planning, software architecture, and ecosystem dynamics. The idea isn't imprisoned in a single document - it's free to participate in multiple conversations across your knowledge base.
## Creating Effective Atomic Notes

A good atomic note is complete enough to make sense on its own, yet focused enough to be genuinely atomic. It typically includes:

- A clear, descriptive title that captures the essence
- The core idea expressed in your own words
- Context or source attribution when relevant
- Links to related atomic notes
- Tags for discovery and organization

The goal isn't brevity for its own sake - it's clarity and independence. A note might be two sentences or two paragraphs, as long as it contains exactly one conceptual unit.

## Building With Atoms

The real magic happens when atomic notes accumulate. Individual atoms combine into molecules, molecules into structures, structures into emergent insights you couldn't have planned. This bottom-up approach mirrors how knowledge actually grows - not through rigid hierarchies, but through organic connection and recombination.

This Wiki embraces the atomic approach, treating each entry as a discrete unit of thought that can stand alone while contributing to larger patterns of understanding.
