==Zettelkasten== is German for 'slip box', originated with sociologist Niklas Luhmann, who used it to produce an extraordinary body of work - over 70 books and 400 articles. His secret wasn't superhuman productivity but a thinking system that turned note-taking into knowledge creation. Each note in his slip box was atomic, linked to related ideas, and capable of spawning new insights through unexpected connections. The system didn't just store his thoughts - it actively participated in developing them, creating a conversation partner that grew more valuable over time.

I'm approaching Zettelkasten as a novice, learning by doing, making mistakes, and discovering patterns as they emerge. This Wiki is my laboratory for exploring these principles - not as rigid doctrine, but as a flexible framework for organising complexity. The atomic notes, the linking structures, the emergent connections - these aren't just organisational tactics. They're a way of thinking that mirrors how ideas actually evolve: through connection, recombination, and the patient accumulation of understanding. Each page here is both content and experiment, a contribution to knowledge and a lesson in knowledge management itself.
